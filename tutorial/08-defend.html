Lesson 8

Explain the choices and their limits

Keep three things separate when you describe the project: what you built, what you measured, and what the data cannot tell you.

A two-minute explanation

I built an audio classifier that estimates whether a Hindi or English speaker has finished a turn. It uses the Whisper Tiny encoder and listens to the final eight seconds. A small classification head turns the pooled encoder output into the probability that the turn is complete. During training, I used label-preserving fillers and sampled difficult duration cases more often.

I compared data policies, pooling methods, silence ranges, encoder freezing, and an audio-plus-text model. F1 alone was not enough because a false complete interrupts the user. A three-seed safety study selected a last-frame model with FCR and recall constraints measured on validation data. On the held-out test set, its FCR was 9.84 percent and its recall was 83.26 percent.

Data is the main limitation. The source has no speaker IDs, transcripts, or verified Hinglish labels, so the Hindi and filler results are proxy measurements. The next useful step is to collect consented human Hinglish speech with speaker identities, rather than make another small change to the classifier.

Questions to answer aloud

Why Whisper Tiny?

It is small and already has multilingual acoustic representations. Keeping only the encoder holds the classifier near 7.9 million parameters.

Why last-frame pooling?

The choice came from the experiments, not intuition alone. E4 performed well in the controlled study and had the best median constrained validation F1 across three seeds.

Why not claim Hinglish accuracy?

The source lacks verified code-switch transcripts. Hindi tags and filler metadata are useful proxy slices, but they cannot establish a true Hinglish benchmark.

What would you improve first?

Collect consented human conversations with speaker IDs, transcripts, and filler and pause annotations. Then create a fixed speaker-disjoint challenge set.
