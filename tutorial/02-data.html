Lesson 2

Know what the dataset can prove

The source is large and multilingual, but size does not repair missing speaker IDs, transcripts, or verified Hinglish labels.

What we used

ItemRecorded value
Full training source270,946 rows, 41.4 GB, 23 language tags
Local source sample7,517 clips
Working split6,613 train, 904 validation
Publisher test sample4,890 held-out clips

The pipeline streams a bounded sample instead of silently downloading 41.4 GB. Each clip becomes mono 16 kHz audio. Long clips keep the last eight seconds because the decision lies near the utterance ending.

Labels stay simple

The publisher endpoint label remains authoritative: complete is 1 and incomplete is 0. Duration, filler metadata, or silence does not automatically relabel a clip. Those signals are imperfect and could encode shortcuts.

Evidence boundary

The source has no speaker or accent identity. Its transcript field is null. A Hindi tag is a proxy, not proof of code-switched Hinglish. The project therefore reports Hindi, filler, and pause slices without calling them a verified human Hinglish benchmark.

Split without speaker IDs

A speaker-disjoint split would be preferable, but it is impossible here. The fallback stratifies by language, label, and source tag. Validation guides model and threshold choices. Test data is opened only after the choice is frozen.

Why not tune on the test set?

Repeated test-driven changes turn the test set into training feedback. Its score then stops estimating behavior on unseen data.
